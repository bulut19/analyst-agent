Returns are killing us. I need to see what's going on.

Which product categories and brands have the highest return rates over the last 12 months (October 2025 to September 2026), among those with enough sales volume to matter, and how has the return rate moved month by month?

Company return rate, Oct 2025 to Sep 2026
28.2%
Returned items as a share of items delivered or returned. Cancelled and not-yet-delivered items are left out.

Findings

  • Suits & Sport Coats (31.4%), Socks (30.7%) and Leggings (30.5%) have the highest return rates among categories with at least 300 items, against a company rate of 28.2%.
  • The category spread is narrow: from Underwear at 23.8% up to Suits & Sport Coats at 31.4%, so no category is far from the average.
  • Only three brands reach 300 items in 12 months: Carhartt (28.2%), Allegra K (28.1%) and Calvin Klein (26.3%). None is clearly above the company rate, so this data does not point to a brand to raise with suppliers.
  • The other 2,714 brands sit below the cut-off and are not ranked, so brand-level problems may be hidden by low volume.
  • The monthly rate moved between 25.2% (July 2026) and 30.8% (November 2025) with no clear upward or downward trend; August and September 2026 are incomplete because returns are still arriving, so those rates are likely understated.
  • Similar categories are kept separate as agreed: Pants (30.0%) and Pants & Capris (29.0%), Socks (30.7%) and Socks & Hosiery (27.9%). The data does not record return reasons or suppliers, so it cannot explain why items come back.

Data notes

I found no high-risk problems. The brief counts items by status and groups them by order month, category and brand, so most of these issues only touch columns we don't use. The two that can change the ranking are the 5 items with no matching product and the 5 misspelled categories. Both are tiny, and both can be fixed with simple rules.

Period: 2025-10-01 to 2026-09-30.

Confirmed with the stakeholder:

  • How many items should a brand or category need before it can be ranked? We expect very few of the 2,756 brands to reach 300 items in 12 months. Answer: 300 for both is fine. That's in line with what I said, and I'd rather see a short list of brands that really matter than a long list of noisy ones.
  • Some categories overlap: Socks and Socks & Hosiery, Pants and Pants & Capris, Suits and Suits & Sport Coats. Should we combine each pair? Answer: Keep them separate as they are in the data for now. I don't want to guess at groupings before a supplier conversation, and I'll check with merchandising if one of them comes up as a candidate.

Decisions assumed:

  • Items are assigned to a month by the order date (order_items.created_at), not the return date.
  • Items still Processing or Shipped are left out, since their outcome is not known.
  • Return rate is counted in items, not orders or money.
  • The company-wide return rate is shown as a reference line on the trend and as a benchmark on the ranking. It uses all items in the window, including low-volume groups, and ignores filters.
  • Where there is nothing to divide by (no items sold), the rate is blank and shown as 'no sales'. The group is not ranked.
  • Null brands are grouped as 'Unknown' and are not ranked as a brand. They stay in totals.
  • Order items whose product does not exist (5 rows) are excluded from every metric so all views add up.
  • The trend shows the overall rate and the rate for any selected category or brand. Aug and Sep 2026 are marked incomplete because recent returns are still arriving. The rate for these months is likely understated.
  • Brand stands in for supplier, because the data has no supplier field. No return reasons are available, so none are shown.
  • Brand spelling variants beyond spaces and case are not merged.
