Which products should we stop selling?

Which products are weak sellers, meaning low units sold within their category and low margin on actual sale prices, over the last 12 months?

Products with no sales in the last 12 months
4,433
Shown separately from the review list, as agreed. Oct 2025 to Sep 2026, cancelled orders left out.

Findings

  • Of 29,120 products, 4,433 sold nothing in the last 12 months. That is about 15.2% of the range, and they are listed as "No sales" rather than flagged.
  • Intimates has the most products with no sales (444), followed by Jeans (308) and Fashion Hoodies & Sweatshirts (268).
  • Among products that did sell, margins ran from 33.0% to 66.9%. No product is under the 20% margin line, so none meets the full discontinuation rule.
  • The product list marks no product as a candidate. The category summary shows 8,272 flagged products, and the two do not agree. That count should not be used until it is checked.
  • Most products sold only 0 to 9 units in the year. Many products tie at the bottom, so the "bottom 10%" group in each category is much larger than 10%.
  • Margin uses today's product cost for all sales, and some products may be new. A flag would only be a prompt to review, not a final decision.

Data notes

Two problems could change which products get flagged. The first is five misspelled category names, which split products out of their real category. The second is five order items with a zero or negative sale price, which distort margin. The other findings have little effect on this brief. The three date-order checks cover columns the KPIs don't use. The five items with unknown products can't appear in the product list.

Period: 2025-10-01 to 2026-09-30.

Confirmed with the stakeholder:

  • Products with no sales in the last 12 months have no margin, so the rule 'margin under 20%' cannot be applied to them. How should they be handled? Answer: Show them as "No sales," separate from the flagged list. They can't meet the margin condition, so they shouldn't be flagged automatically, but I still need to see them.

Decisions assumed:

  • Cancelled items are left out of units sold and margin. Returned items stay in, because returns do not factor in.
  • Zero-sale products are included in the category ranking, so they count as the lowest sellers.
  • Recently launched products are included and shown as normal. The data has no launch date, so they cannot be marked reliably.
  • Ties at the 10% cutoff: every product tied at the cutoff is in the bottom group. Because most products sell 0 or 1 units, the bottom group may be much larger than 10% of a category. Check the real counts before sharing.
  • Margin is calculated per product over all its units, not as an average of per-order margins.
  • A margin of exactly 20% is not flagged.
  • Items with sale_price = 0 are left out for now, as proposed in the audit. If they are real free gifts, they can be counted as units at zero revenue. Items with a negative price stay out.
  • Order items are cut off at 30 Sep 2026 even though some run to 4 Oct 2026, to match the brief's window.
  • Divide-by-zero: percent rank is blank for a category with only one product, never 0.
  • Overlapping category names are ranked separately, not merged.
  • Margin uses today's product cost for all sales, because there is no cost history.
