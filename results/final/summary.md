## Check-in questions (asked after the metric plan, before any SQL)

- **marketing:** If only part of an order was returned, should the whole order be left out? Left out means it can't count as a first order or a second order. Options: Yes, leave out any order with a returned item; No, only leave out orders marked Returned as a whole. I'd suggest: Yes, leave out any order with a returned item, unless you prefer otherwise. (It changes which customers count as new or repeat buyers, so it moves each channel's repeat rate.) *Answer:* No, only leave out orders that were fully returned or cancelled. If someone kept part of an order, they still made a real purchase, so it should count as a first or repeat order.
- **marketing:** If a customer places two separate orders on the same day, should the second one count as a repeat purchase? Options: No, a repeat must be on a later day; Yes, any second order counts, even on the same day. I'd suggest: No, a repeat must be on a later day, unless you prefer otherwise. (Same-day orders can look like a split basket, not a return visit, so counting them could raise the repeat rate for some channels.) *Answer:* No, a repeat should be on a later day. Two orders on the same day are really one shopping trip, not a customer coming back.
- **merchandising:** Products with no sales in the last 12 months have no margin, so the rule 'margin under 20%' cannot be applied to them. How should they be handled? Options: Show them as 'No sales', separate from flagged candidates; Flag them automatically as candidates; Judge them on list-price margin (retail price vs cost) and flag if under 20%. I'd suggest: Show them as 'No sales', separate from flagged candidates, unless you prefer otherwise. (Many products probably sold nothing, so this choice decides whether the weakest products appear in the flagged list and changes the headline candidate count a lot.) *Answer:* Show them as "No sales," separate from the flagged list. They can't meet the margin condition, so they shouldn't be flagged automatically, but I still need to see them.
- **repeat_customers:** Should cancelled orders be ignored when finding a customer's first and second order? Returned orders would still count. Options: Ignore cancelled orders (returned still count); Count cancelled orders too; Ignore both cancelled and returned orders. I'd suggest: Ignore cancelled orders (returned still count), unless you prefer otherwise. (It changes who counts as a first-time customer, which year they fall in, and the repeat rate headline number.) *Answer:* Ignore cancelled orders and keep returned ones counted. That's the standard way to measure real purchases, so go with your suggestion.
- **repeat_customers:** For the recent groups that do not have 12 months of history yet (Oct 2025 onward), how should the rate be shown? Options: Rate so far only, labeled 'not final'; Also add a same-time comparison, such as the 6-month repeat rate for every year; Hide these groups until complete. I'd suggest: Rate so far only, labeled 'not final', unless you prefer otherwise. (Without a same-time comparison, 2025 Q4 and 2026 will look lower than earlier years just because they have had less time to reorder.) *Answer:* Go with the same-time comparison, such as the 6-month repeat rate for every year, along with the "not final" labels. I need to know whether 2026 really dropped, and without a like-for-like measure the recent groups will look worse just because they've had less time to reorder.
- **returns:** How many items should a brand or category need before it can be ranked? We expect very few of the 2,756 brands to reach 300 items in 12 months. Options: 300 items for both categories and brands; 300 for categories, 100 for brands; 300 for categories, and for brands the 50 with the most items sold. I'd suggest: 300 items for both categories and brands, unless you prefer otherwise. (A higher cut-off may leave only a handful of brands on the ranking, and a lower one adds more brands but with noisier rates.) *Answer:* 300 for both is fine. That's in line with what I said, and I'd rather see a short list of brands that really matter than a long list of noisy ones.
- **returns:** Some categories overlap: Socks and Socks & Hosiery, Pants and Pants & Capris, Suits and Suits & Sport Coats. Should we combine each pair? Options: Keep them separate, as they are in the data; Combine the three obvious pairs; Let merchandising give us the mapping first. I'd suggest: Keep them separate, as they are in the data, unless you prefer otherwise. (Combining adds their volume together, which can lift a category over the 300 cut-off and change its rank and rate.) *Answer:* Keep them separate as they are in the data for now. I don't want to guess at groupings before a supplier conversation, and I'll check with merchandising if one of them comes up as a candidate.
