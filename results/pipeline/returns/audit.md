# Data Quality Audit

The biggest problem is the shipping, delivery and return dates. They often come before the order date, and this affects about 34,000 rows. This would put items in the wrong month if we used those dates, so I suggest placing items by order date. The other problems are small (5 rows each). They are the category spellings, the products missing from the product list, and the odd prices. I have proposed a simple fix for each.

## Issues to fix

| Severity | Table.column | Rows | What is wrong | Proposed rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| medium | `order_items.created_at, shipped_at` | 33,766 | About 34,000 items show a ship date before the order date. An item cannot ship before it is ordered, so the ship date cannot be trusted. The brief says items are placed in a month by order date or ship date. If we used ship date, many items would land in the wrong month, and the monthly trend would be distorted. Item counts overall would not change. | Place every item in a month using created_at (order date) only. Do not use shipped_at for any KPI. Do not drop these rows. Decide whether an item counts as returned or delivered from the status field, not from the dates. |
| low | `products.category` | 5 | Five products have a category spelled differently, such as 'jeans ' with a trailing space, 'Sweater', 'Tops and Tees' and 'Accesories'. They would show up as separate tiny categories. Their items would be missing from the real category and would fail the 100-item minimum. Only five products are affected, so the effect is small. | Trim spaces and ignore case on products.category. Then map: JEANS and 'jeans ' to Jeans, 'Tops and Tees' to 'Tops & Tees', Sweater to Sweaters, Accesories to Accessories. |
| low | `order_items.product_id` | 5 | Five order items point to product IDs (30001 to 30005) that are not in the product list. We cannot tell their category or brand. They would drop out of the category and brand views, but could still count in the company-wide rate. That would make the comparison slightly inconsistent. The effect is tiny. | Exclude order items whose product_id is not in products.id from all KPIs, including the company-wide rate, so every number uses the same base. Keep a count of excluded items and show it in a footnote. |
| low | `order_items.sale_price` | 5 | Five items have a zero or negative sale price. This looks like bad or test data. No KPI in this brief uses price, because they are all item counts. The risk is only that these might be non-genuine items counted as sales. With five rows, the effect is negligible. | Exclude order items with sale_price <= 0 from all item counts, since they are likely invalid rows. Log how many were removed. |
| low | `order_items.created_at, delivered_at` | 5,761 | About 5,800 items show a delivery date before the order date. The dates are wrong, but the brief decides delivered or returned by status, not by delivery date. So the item counts are not affected unless we use delivery date to place items in a month. | Do not use delivered_at for any KPI or month placement. Use the status field to decide delivered, returned or cancelled. Keep the rows. |
| low | `order_items.created_at, returned_at` | 637 | 637 items show a return date before the order date. Some of them cross a month boundary, for example ordered on 1 Jan and returned on 31 Dec. If we placed returns by return date, those would land in the wrong month. Placing items by order date avoids this. The count of returned items is unchanged. | Do not use returned_at to place items in a month. Use created_at. Count an item as returned from the status field. Keep the rows. |

## Decisions needed

- **order_items.created_at, shipped_at:** Can we place each item in its month by the date it was ordered? The ship date is unreliable, and the return date is also sometimes wrong. The trade-off is that a return shows in the month of the order, not the month the customer sent it back.
