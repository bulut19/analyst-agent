# Data Quality Audit

Several small data problems could distort the repeat-rate numbers. The worst are duplicate order IDs, orders with no customer, orders dated in the future, and orders with absurd item counts. Delivery, ship and return timestamp order problems, email duplicates, and the Kagawa and Austria flags don't affect this brief. The product category misspellings should be fixed before any category breakdown.

## Issues to fix

| Severity | Table.column | Rows | What is wrong | Proposed rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| high | `orders.order_id` | 5 | Five rows share an order ID with another row. Repeat buying counts separate orders. A duplicated order could make a one-time buyer look like a repeat buyer. It could also double count items in the first order. | Check whether duplicate rows are exact copies. If so, keep one row per order_id. If they differ, keep the earliest-created row and flag the rest. Then count distinct order_id per customer. |
| high | `orders.created_at` | 5 | Five orders are dated 2027 or 2028. Data ends in September 2026, so these dates are wrong. Such an order could wrongly make a customer a repeat buyer, or push a first order into the wrong year group. The true date is unknown. | Exclude orders with created_at later than the data cutoff (30 Sep 2026) from all KPIs. Log them as excluded. |
| medium | `orders.user_id` | 5 | Five orders have no customer. They can't be linked to anyone, so they can't count toward a customer's first or second order. They are very few, so the effect is small. | Exclude orders where user_id is null from all customer-level KPIs. Do not guess the customer. |
| medium | `orders.num_of_item` | 5 | Five orders claim 120 to 999 items, while 99% of orders have 4 or fewer. These are almost certainly entry errors. Order counts don't use item numbers, so the KPIs barely move. The breakdowns by category, brand and department could still be skewed if these orders are first orders. | Flag orders with num_of_item > 40. Check them against the actual order_items row count. Exclude them from the first-order category, department and brand breakdowns if the counts don't match. Keep them as orders for the repeat rate only if the customer and date are valid. |
| medium | `products.category` | 5 | A few product categories are spelled in different ways, for example 'JEANS', 'jeans ', 'Sweater' and 'Accesories'. In the category breakdown, the same category would show as separate groups. This would split the groups and hide real differences. Only five products are affected. | Standardise category: trim spaces, ignore case, and map 'Tops and Tees' to 'Tops & Tees', 'Sweater' to 'Sweaters', 'Accesories' to 'Accessories', and 'JEANS' and 'jeans ' to 'Jeans'. |
| medium | `order_items.product_id` | 5 | Five order items point to product IDs 30001 to 30005 that don't exist in the product table. These items have no category, brand or department. They would drop out of or show as unknown in those breakdowns. The order itself is still valid for the repeat count. | Keep the orders in the repeat-rate counts. Label the items 'Unknown product' in the category, department and brand breakdowns. Do not drop the orders. |
| low | `order_items.sale_price` | 5 | Five items have a zero or negative sale price. None of the KPIs use price. A price of zero or less is not valid for a sale. It only matters if a revenue view is added later. | Do not use sale_price in this dashboard. If price is added later, exclude order items with sale_price <= 0. Keep the orders in the repeat counts. |
| low | `orders.created_at, delivered_at` | 5 | Five orders show delivery before the order was placed. This is impossible. The brief doesn't use delivery dates, and the order date is still usable. The 76570 example has the delivery time exactly 10 days before creation, so the delivery date is probably the wrong one. | Do not use delivered_at in this dashboard. Set delivered_at to null where it is before created_at, if delivery is used later. |
| low | `orders.shipped_at, delivered_at` | 5 | Five orders show delivery before shipping. They are the same orders as the delivery-before-order-date problem in finding 7, apart from small differences. The brief uses neither date. | Do not use shipped_at or delivered_at in this dashboard. Set both to null on these orders if delivery is used later. |

## Flagged but not a problem

| Verdict | Table.column | Rows | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| expected | `order_items.created_at, returned_at` | 637 | Return timestamps come before the item's created time by a few hours in about 637 rows. The gaps are small and look like timestamp noise. The brief doesn't use return dates, and returned orders still count. |
| expected | `order_items.created_at, shipped_at` | 33,766 | Item-level shipping times before the item created time are common (33,766 rows). This looks like how the item timestamps were built, with order and item times not aligned. It is not a business error. The KPIs use order dates, not item ship dates. |
| expected | `order_items.created_at, delivered_at` | 5,761 | Item-level delivery times before the item created time (5,761 rows) are the same kind of timestamp mismatch as in finding 8. The KPIs don't use item delivery dates. |
| expected | `users.email` | 25,534 | Many customers share an email address (25,534 rows). The data is synthetic, and the user ID is the real customer key. The KPIs count customers by user ID, not email. Do not merge customers by email. |
| false alarm | `users.state` | 5 | Kagawa and Kanagawa are two different real prefectures in Japan. Kagawa is not a misspelling. |
| false alarm | `users.country` | 2 | Austria and Australia are two different countries. Austria is not a misspelling. Merging them would put customers in the wrong location. |
