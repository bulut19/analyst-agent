# Data Quality Audit

The data is usable for the return-rate dashboard once two things are fixed. First, merge 5 misspelled category names. Second, handle 5 order items whose product can't be found. The odd dates and the few zero or negative prices don't change the return-rate counts, because they don't use those fields. I suggest we use order date and item status only, and warn the stakeholder that the date fields are unreliable.

## Issues to fix

| Severity | Table.column | Rows | What is wrong | Proposed rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| medium | `products.category` | 5 | Five products have category names spelled differently from the main one (JEANS, 'jeans ', Tops and Tees, Sweater, Accesories). Left alone, they would show up as separate tiny categories. The 500-item cut-off would then drop them. Their items would be missing from the real categories, so those categories' counts and rates would be slightly off. These are clearly typos, not new categories. | Clean category before grouping. Trim spaces, ignore upper/lower case, and map: JEANS and 'jeans ' to 'Jeans'; 'Tops and Tees' to 'Tops & Tees'; 'Sweater' to 'Sweaters'; 'Accesories' to 'Accessories'. Then re-run the check to confirm no rare variants remain. |
| low | `order_items.product_id` | 5 | Five order items point to product IDs (30001–30005) that don't exist in the products table. We can't tell their category or brand. They can't be placed in any category or brand view. Five items won't move any rate noticeably, but they would make the company total differ from the sum of the categories. | Exclude order items whose product_id is not in products from every view, including the company-wide benchmark, so totals match. Log the count of excluded rows. Ask the data owner whether those products were deleted. |
| low | `order_items.sale_price` | 5 | Five items have a zero or negative sale price (for example -120). A price can't be negative, so these are wrong or are refund/test entries. The return-rate KPIs only count items and don't use price, so they aren't affected. The rows would matter if we later add revenue or refund value. | Keep these rows in the item counts, because status decides delivered or returned. Exclude items with sale_price <= 0 from any future money-based measure. Note that the dashboard has no value figures. |
| low | `order_items.created_at, returned_at` | 637 | 637 items show a return time before the order time, which is impossible. The brief assigns items to months by order date and counts a return by status. So the counts are not affected. The problem would matter if the stakeholder chooses return date for the month instead. Then those items could land in the wrong month. | Define 'returned' from the item status, not the date fields. Assign month by created_at. Don't drop these rows. If return-date timing is chosen later, exclude rows where returned_at < created_at from that view. |
| low | `order_items.created_at, shipped_at` | 33,766 | About 33,800 items show shipping before the order was placed. That is impossible, and it is a large share of the data. It suggests the timestamps were generated or recorded unreliably. The dashboard doesn't use shipping time. The only risk is that the order date is also off by a day or two, which would only move items near a month boundary. | Don't use shipped_at in any KPI. Don't exclude rows. Use created_at for the month and status for delivered/returned. Tell the data owner about the date problem. |
| low | `order_items.created_at, delivered_at` | 5,761 | About 5,800 items show delivery before the order was placed. This is the same kind of timestamp problem as shipping dates. The KPIs use item status and order date, so they are not affected. Do not use delivered_at for anything in this dashboard. | Don't use delivered_at in any KPI or filter. Identify delivered items by status. Don't exclude rows. |
