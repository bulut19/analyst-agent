## Judge comments

- **marketing:** Main strength: the brief covers the real need. It names the budget-shift decision, defines the 12-month repeat rate precisely, sets a monthly view over two years by channel, notes the CMO as audience, and explains the missing spend data, the attribution assumption and the small-cohort risk. Main gap: the 'last 2 years' window (Oct 2023–Sep 2025) still needs the requester to confirm it, and the brief never lists the actual channels, so a manager would likely ask for one quick change before signing off.
- **returns:** The brief gets the decision (which categories and brands to review with suppliers), the return-rate definition with delivered-or-returned items as the denominator, the last 12 months with a monthly trend, and the category and brand breakdowns, and it is open about assumptions and data gaps. Its main gap is that the volume cutoff covers brands only and shows every category, which misses the stakeholder's focus on categories with enough volume; several basics are also still left for the stakeholder to confirm, such as whether items are dated by order or return date and how to treat the partial latest month, and the audience question added little.
- **finance:** The brief covers the decision and gets the Revenue exclusions and gross margin % right. It also covers the month and quarter views, the 2026 year-to-date versus 2025 comparison, and it is honest about its assumptions, including the date basis and the partial Q3. The main gap is the Men/Women split: the analyst never asked about it, and the brief defaults to the customer's gender instead of the product's department. It flags this, but the default is wrong, so the brief needs a change before sign-off.
- **merchandising:** The brief captures the full need: the bottom 10% by units within each category combined with margin below 20% over 12 months, units, revenue and margin shown per product, and the link to next season's buying plan. It used four well-chosen questions and is candid about limits like zero-sales products, part-year launches and ties. The main gaps are that the 12-month window is still open, and 'rounded to whole products' for the 10% cutoff doesn't say whether to round up, down or to the nearest product, so two analysts could flag different products. A manager would also have to sign off on the brand and department additions nobody asked for.
- **repeat_customers:** The brief defines the 12-month repeat purchase rate by first-order year, splits US from international, and states the limits plainly: 2025 and 2026 groups are marked incomplete, and a lower 2026 figure alone does not prove a drop. Its main gap is that it never offers a fair way to compare 2026 with earlier years (for example, measuring every group over the same number of months since the first order), so it may not answer whether repeat purchases dropped; it also leaves out a by-country view and never says outright that 'no drop' is a valid result.
